# Wi-Fi troubleshooting

## Cannot connect to the network

If a laptop cannot join the office wireless network, first confirm that Wi-Fi is enabled and airplane mode is disabled. Check that the device is within the normal coverage area and that the selected network is the approved employee network rather than a guest network.

Forget the saved network only when the helpdesk asks you to do so, then reconnect using the current credentials. Do not try networks with similar names because an incorrectly selected network may be operated by an unknown party.

If other devices connect successfully, restart the affected laptop once and try again. Record whether the failure occurs before or after the sign-in prompt.

## Slow connection

A slow wireless connection can be caused by distance from an access point, local interference, a large download, or a temporary service issue. Move closer to the normal work area and pause large transfers before testing again.

Compare the result with another approved device in the same location. A single slow speed test does not establish that the wireless service is unavailable.

When reporting the issue, include the room or floor, approximate time, device type, and whether wired access works.

## Reconnecting after sleep

Some laptops take a short time to restore wireless access after waking from sleep. Wait briefly, confirm that the wireless icon has settled, and open an approved internal page.

If the connection remains unavailable, turn Wi-Fi off and on once. Avoid repeatedly changing adapter settings because this can remove managed configuration.

## Capturing useful evidence

A useful support report states the network name, location, time, and the exact message shown by the operating system. Include whether the problem affects one device or several nearby devices.

A screenshot should exclude personal messages, account details, and unrelated open windows.

## When to contact support

Contact the helpdesk if the device repeatedly loses access, cannot see the employee network, or displays a certificate warning.

Do not accept an unexpected certificate prompt to make the connection work. If the issue affects several people in one area, mention that in the ticket so support can investigate a shared access point or service condition.
